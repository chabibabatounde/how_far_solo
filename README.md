A dominant trend in metaheuristic design is to enrich well-established algorithms with increasingly elaborate adaptive mechanisms, often at the cost of extra hyperparameters, higher computational overhead, and reduced interpretability. This raises a simple question: is this added complexity always needed to stay competitive? We explore this question by introducing SOLO (Single-schedule Optimizer with Leader-guided Operators), a deliberately minimal baseline with no explicit memory and no higher-order adaptive mechanism. SOLO coordinates three operators: leader-guided exploitation, coordinate-wise local search, and Lévy-flight restart on stagnation, through a single exponentially shaped schedule, driven by one hyperparameter $k$. We evaluate SOLO under a limited evaluation budget against nine established algorithms on the CEC-2017 ($D \in \{30,50,100\}$) and CEC-2022 ($D \in \{10,20\}$) benchmark suites, as well as on 56 constrained real-world engineering problems from CEC-2020-RW. SOLO achieves genuine competitiveness against far more sophisticated algorithms, with its gap to LSHADEcnEpSin narrowing as dimensionality increases, and it ranks first among ten algorithms on the mechanical engineering subset of CEC-2020-RW, using only a static external penalty. Its performance drops, however, on on CEC-2022 at $D=20$  and high-dimensional and heavily constrained problems (the Power Systems and Feed Ration families of CEC-2020-RW), where its single, budget-driven progress variable gives no signal about problem difficulty. An ablation study confirms that leader-guided exploitation and local search are statistically essential components, while a sensitivity analysis shows that the optimal value of $k$ increases with landscape complexity. These results suggest that a simple, single-hyperparameter optimizer can go remarkably far, with its main limitation being identifiable and fixable rather than structural.
